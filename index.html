This is a public preview of high school biology curriculum materials in active development at AISC. The pages linked below are the actual student-facing interactives — what students see when they open a section. The page architecture, the design lenses, the pedagogy, and the deployment model are all on display.

Students at AISC reach this material through Google Sites pages that embed each section's Apps Script web app. The HTML you see in the previews here is the same HTML those Apps Script web apps serve. This Vercel-hosted site exists so anyone — colleagues, other teachers, curriculum designers — can see what the work looks like without needing an AISC account.

Four design lenses

Every page is built against these constraints. They aren't features to bolt on — they decide what counts as a good piece of student material.

Phenomenon-driven Each section opens with a real, local, unresolved question — not a vocabulary list.
AI-literacy integrated Every section has a structured AI critique task with deliberately flawed AI text we authored.
Lab-first / data-first Assessment focuses on students interpreting their own data — the kind a chatbot can't fetch.
Self-paced interactive HTML The page is the lesson. Reading, models, and practice live in one URL.

For colleagues

The page architecture is intentionally portable. The interactive HTML, the shared CSS/JS, the Apps Script deployment pattern, the Google Sheet content schema — all in the public repo at github.com/matthewignash/aisc-g9-biology. If you want to adapt any of this for your own school, fork it.

Why this redesign

A 2025–26 student survey at AISC found that science is the class where 9th graders use AI the most. The honest interpretation is that current materials aren't meeting student needs — students are filling the gap with AI tutors. The traditional pattern (lecture slides → reading → worksheets → test) inherits from a different teacher and is not what I want to continue.

The redesign specifies a student-facing experience that is hard to shortcut with AI, engaging enough that students don't reach for AI first, and deployable inside AISC's IT environment. The full rationale is in the Unit 2 design brief.
